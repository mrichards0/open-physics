(a) Alice moves north at 6 knots and Bob west at 6 knots. Are their velocity vectors equal?

No; equal magnitudes do not compensate for different directions.Yes; both speeds are 6 knots.Yes; north and west are equivalent vector directions.

(b) Alice moves west at 6 knots and Bob west at 3 knots. Equal?

No; their directions match but magnitudes differ.Yes; both point west.No; their directions differ.

(c) Alice moves northeast at 6 knots and Bob south at 3 knots. Equal?

No; both magnitude and direction differ.Yes; both are moving.No; only their magnitudes differ.

(d) Alice moves northeast at 6 knots and Bob southwest at 6 knots. Equal?

No; they are antiparallel, not equal.Yes; magnitudes are equal.Yes; opposite directions make equal vectors.

(e) Both move northeast at 2 knots, though at different locations. Equal?

Yes; vectors depend on magnitude and direction, not where drawn.No; vectors at different locations cannot be equal.No; northeast is not a vector direction.

Two vectors are equal exactly when both magnitude and direction match. Their drawing locations do not affect equality.
