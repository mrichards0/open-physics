Why does requiring all terms in an equation to have the same dimensions also force arguments of standard power-series functions to be dimensionless?

A standard series contains a dimensionless constant term and powers x^n added together; matching every term to the constant requires every x^n, and therefore x, to be dimensionless.Any variable raised to a power automatically becomes dimensionless.The coefficients supply whatever dimensions are needed separately for every term.Arguments may have arbitrary dimensions because addition does not require matching dimensions.

In a standard series a0+a1x+a2x^2+..., the coefficients are dimensionless and a0 is dimensionless. Terms can be added only if x, x^2, and all later powers are dimensionless. Thus the argument x must be dimensionless.
