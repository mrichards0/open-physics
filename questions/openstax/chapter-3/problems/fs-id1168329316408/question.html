A dragster reaches {{params.v}} m/s from rest in {{params.t}} s. (a) Find its average acceleration. (b) If that acceleration were incorrectly treated as constant over {{params.d}} m, find the predicted final speed. (c) Explain why this can exceed the measured speed.

A real dragster's acceleration is not constant; it is typically larger early and falls as speed rises, so extending the time-average acceleration over the full distance overpredicts speed.The measured speed must be wrong because constant acceleration is required by kinematics.The predicted value is larger only because meters and seconds are incompatible units.

a_avg=v/t. The constant-a extrapolation gives v_pred=sqrt(2a_avg d), but a time average from the launch interval need not describe acceleration over the whole track.
